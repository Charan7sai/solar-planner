See what solar panels on your roof would cost and save

Click your roof on a satellite map. A segmentation model outlines it and measures the area. The planner then uses 20 years of NASA weather data for that exact spot to estimate how much power panels would generate, what they cost after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, and how many years they take to pay for themselves.

How it works

Three steps, about two minutes

You need your pincode and a recent electricity bill. Everything else comes from satellite data.

  1. Find your building

    Enter your pincode, add the street if you know it, or use your phone's location. Search takes you to the right area. You then move the map to your own roof, because address data in India rarely points to a single building.

  2. Outline the roof

    Press Detect roof and click the middle of your roof. The model draws the outline and measures it. If the outline is off, drag its corners, or trace the roof yourself.

  3. Enter your electricity use

    Add the monthly units from your bill and your tariff. You get a recommended system size, generation for each month, the cost after subsidy, the payback period and savings over 25 years.

The estimate

What the planner works out for you

  • System size in kW and the number of 550 W panels, limited by your roof space or by how much power you use.
  • Generation in a typical year and in a poor year, from 20 real years of weather at your location.
  • Cost before and after central financial assistance under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana.
  • Net metering for each month: units you use save your full tariff, and surplus units earn the export rate.
  • Payback period and savings over 25 years, including panel ageing of 0.5% a year, tariff rises of 3% a year and upkeep of 1% of the cost a year.
  • Carbon dioxide avoided each year, using the India grid average of 0.71 kg per unit.

Accuracy and limits

How far you can trust the numbers

The roof model was trained on 21,000 aerial images from 15 countries. It was then tested on 300 buildings in places it had never seen, by simulating a click in the middle of each roof and comparing the outline with the real one.

Known limits

  • It works best where imagery is sharp, mostly in cities. In smaller towns the planner falls back to lower resolution imagery and tells you so.
  • Dense blocks of similar grey flat roofs are hard. The model may miss the roof or join it to a neighbour's. Trace these by hand.
  • Shade from trees, water tanks and nearby buildings is not modelled yet.
  • This is an estimate, not a quote. Get a site survey from an installer before you buy.

FAQ

Common questions

Is it free?

Yes. You do not need an account, and there is nothing to pay. The planner runs on free hosting and free public data.

How accurate is the roof area?

On buildings it had never seen, the median error was 21%. Always check the outline on the map and drag its corners if needed. You can also type the area yourself.

Where does the sunlight data come from?

From NASA POWER, which provides sunlight and temperature records from satellites for any point on Earth. The planner uses monthly values from 2004 to 2023 for your coordinates.

How is the subsidy calculated?

It follows central financial assistance for homes under PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to ₹78,000. Extra subsidies from states are not included. Check the latest rules before applying.

Does it store my location or address?

No. Address search goes from your browser to the OpenStreetMap Nominatim service. For roof detection, imagery dates and estimates, your coordinates are sent to this site's server, used for that request and not saved. Anonymous usage counts, such as how many estimates were run, are logged without any location.

How recent is the satellite imagery?

It depends on the area. Esri updates its imagery several times a year, and big cities get new pictures most often. In late 2026, Hyderabad showed imagery from February 2026 and Pune from April 2026. The planner shows the capture date under the map for wherever you are looking. Newer imagery exists only from paid commercial providers.

Why did roof detection fail?

Usually for one of three reasons: the map is not zoomed in enough (zoom to level 17 or closer), there is no sharp imagery for your area, or the roof is hard to see because it is very dark, very small or under trees. The planner tells you which one it was and what to do next. You can always trace the roof by hand.

Can I use it outside India?

The generation estimate works anywhere. The subsidy, rupee prices and pincode search are for India.

Ready to check your roof?

Keep your pincode and last electricity bill handy.
